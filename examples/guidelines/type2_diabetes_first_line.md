# Type 2 Diabetes — Initial Pharmacologic Therapy

Management of type 2 diabetes combines lifestyle measures with pharmacologic therapy guided by
individualized glycemic targets, comorbidities, and patient preference. A typical HbA1c target for
many nonpregnant adults is below 7 percent, relaxed for older adults or those with limited life
expectancy or hypoglycemia risk.

## Metformin and early combination

Metformin has historically been the preferred initial glucose-lowering medication for many patients
because of efficacy, low cost, and a favorable safety profile. It is continued unless contraindicated
or not tolerated.

## Organ-protective agents

In patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney
disease, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) or a sodium-glucose cotransporter-2
inhibitor (SGLT2 inhibitor) with proven benefit is recommended independent of HbA1c and independent of
metformin use. SGLT2 inhibitors are favored when heart failure or chronic kidney disease predominates,
while GLP-1 receptor agonists are favored when atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease predominates or
when weight management is a priority.

## Weight and hypoglycemia considerations

When minimizing hypoglycemia is a priority, agents such as GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors,
or DPP-4 inhibitors are preferred over sulfonylureas and insulin. When weight loss is a priority,
GLP-1 receptor agonists with greater weight effect are preferred.
