# Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) — Outpatient Adults

Community-acquired pneumonia is a lower respiratory tract infection acquired outside of a
hospital setting. Diagnosis is supported by an acute cough, fever, dyspnea, or pleuritic chest
pain together with a new infiltrate on chest imaging when imaging is obtained.

## Severity assessment

Validated severity tools such as CURB-65 or the Pneumonia Severity Index help decide between
outpatient and inpatient care. Healthy outpatients with no major comorbidities and a low severity
score can typically be treated at home with oral antibiotics.

## Empiric outpatient antibiotic therapy

For previously healthy adults without recent antibiotic use and no comorbidities, recommended
first-line options include amoxicillin at a high dose, doxycycline, or a macrolide such as
azithromycin in regions where pneumococcal macrolide resistance is low.

For outpatients with comorbidities such as chronic heart, lung, liver, or renal disease, diabetes,
or asplenia, a respiratory fluoroquinolone (for example levofloxacin or moxifloxacin) used alone,
or the combination of a beta-lactam (such as amoxicillin-clavulanate) plus a macrolide, is preferred.

## Duration and follow-up

Most patients are treated for a minimum of five days and should be afebrile for 48 to 72 hours and
clinically stable before stopping antibiotics. Patients who do not improve within 48 to 72 hours
should be reassessed for complications, resistant organisms, or an alternative diagnosis.
