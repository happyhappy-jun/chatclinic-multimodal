# Sepsis — Early Recognition and Resuscitation

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.
Septic shock is a subset with circulatory and cellular metabolic abnormalities, clinically identified
by persisting hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain a mean arterial pressure of 65 mmHg and a
serum lactate above 2 mmol/L despite adequate volume resuscitation.

## First-hour priorities

Early management emphasizes prompt recognition and treatment. Measure lactate, obtain blood cultures
before antibiotics when this does not cause substantial delay, and administer broad-spectrum
antibiotics rapidly. For sepsis-induced hypoperfusion or septic shock, begin intravenous crystalloid,
commonly at least 30 mL/kg, within the first three hours, with reassessment of fluid responsiveness.

## Vasopressors

Norepinephrine is the recommended first-line vasopressor for septic shock. Vasopressin or epinephrine
may be added when the target mean arterial pressure is not achieved with norepinephrine alone. The
initial mean arterial pressure target is 65 mmHg.

## Source control and reassessment

Identify and control the anatomic source of infection as soon as feasible. Reassess hemodynamic status
frequently using clinical examination and dynamic measures rather than static pressures alone, and
de-escalate antibiotics once pathogen and susceptibility data are available.
